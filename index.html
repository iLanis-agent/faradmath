Farad Math

A 100 µF capacitor at 12 V holds 1200 µC and 7.2 mJ. In series with a 47 µF one, the pair acts like 31.97 µF and the smaller one takes 8.16 V.

Boundary: arithmetic only Farad Math works out capacitance, charge, energy and voltage splits from the numbers you type. The capacitances and the voltage are yours to measure or read off your own circuit. It treats every capacitor as exact, full and ideal, so it has not seen your circuit and does not know the parts' tolerance, leakage or age, how they were charged before, the source's behavior or what anything is rated for, and it gives no verdict that a circuit is adequate or safe. A charged capacitor can hold energy after power is off; every number here is your own entry.

Starting examples: a 100 µF capacitor at 12 V, then 100 µF and 47 µF in parallel and in series.

The charge truth

A 100 µF capacitor at 12 V holds 1200 µC of charge and 7.2 mJ of energy.

The parallel truth

100 µF and 47 µF in parallel act like 147 µF, holding 1764 µC at 12 V.

The series truth

100 µF and 47 µF in series act like 31.97 µF. At 12 V the pair carries 383.67 µC, with 3.84 V on the 100 µF one and 8.16 V on the 47 µF one.
